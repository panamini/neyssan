Reworked Grayscale Dark Mode Palette

This palette has been adjusted to avoid pure black (#000000) and pure white (#FFFFFF) for better long-term usability and eye comfort, starting with a soft dark gray background inspired by apps like Notion (#121212, approximated here to #0E0E0E from golden ratio values for harmony). It uses the golden ratio sequence for gray levels (0, 14, 37, 60, 97, 158, 195, 218, 241, 255), but clipped to avoid extremes: #0E0E0E to #F1F1F1. All contrasts between background/surface and text elements meet or exceed WCAG 4.5:1 (e.g., #0E0E0E bg with #DADADA text is ~15:1, ensuring readability in web design). Ideal for programming/math/science interfaces—peaceful, silent, and focused.

Reworked Muted Gray-Tinted Palette (Gris Coloré)

This muted, gray-tinted palette ("gris coloré" interpreted as subtly colored grays) has been refined for universality and peacefulness, using a central gray base (#616161 from golden ratio 97) with Fibonacci increments (13, 21, 34, 55, 89, 144) applied to one RGB channel at a time, wrapping at 255 for harmony. It creates earthy, low-saturation tints evoking science and open-source calm—subtle reds, greens, blues infused into grays. Low saturation prevents fatigue, suitable for long-term weekday programming workflows.

Grey with One or Two Color Accents Palette

Replacing the pop 80s palette, this is a sober gray-dominant scheme with just one or two subtle accents (a calm blue and green from golden ratio cores) for actions, ensuring they catch the eye without overwhelming. Based on the reworked grayscale above, accents use RGB combinations from 0/97/158 for low saturation. Universal for math/science sites—peaceful grays for content, accents for interactive elements like buttons or code highlights. Accents are desaturated to avoid saturation fatigue.

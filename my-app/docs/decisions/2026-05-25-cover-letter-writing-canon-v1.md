# CoverLetterWritingCanonV1

The truth plan is for the machine. The letter is for the reader.

## Contract

Strong cover letters should be grounded, concise, specific, recruiter-safe, and human. They should use source-backed examples and brief story logic, not visible ATS keyword stuffing or a mechanical `role requires X -> candidate has X -> therefore fit` rhythm.

## Opening modes

- `proof_led`: start with the candidate's strongest backed proof, metric, or achievement.
- `through_line`: start with a compact thread connecting backed work across roles.
- `candidate_work_context`: start with the candidate's actual work surface before naming fit.
- `adjacent_boundary`: for adjacent-only cases, state the supported adjacent surface and boundary honestly.
- `no_context_work_surface`: for no-context cases, start from the role's concrete work surface without candidate-history claims.

## Opening rules

- `normal` / strong evidence: prefer `proof_led`, `through_line`, or `candidate_work_context`.
- `adjacent_only`: use `adjacent_boundary`.
- `no_context_safe`: use `no_context_work_surface`.
- Strong evidence letters should not start by paraphrasing the role.

## Hard-fail openings

- `I am excited to apply`
- `I'm excited to apply`
- `I am writing to express my interest`
- `Your role requires`
- `My background aligns with`
- `The role's focus aligns with`
- `What interests me about this role`

## Warning terms

Warn on overuse of: `aligns with`, `opportunity`, `contribute`, `passion`, `compelling`, `excited`.

## Golden target: employment-strong-frontend

Dear Hiring Manager,

At BrightLayer, I worked on the kind of frontend foundation that matters once a product is already in customers' hands: React and TypeScript work, a design system migration used across 4 product squads, and bundle and rendering optimizations that reduced page load time by 28 percent.

That was not only component cleanup. It meant making reusable UI patterns easier for product squads to ship, keeping performance visible, and staying close to product and design decisions. At Northline Labs, I built experimentation dashboards used by product and growth teams and partnered directly with design on customer-facing workflow improvements.

Those projects are the through-line I would bring here: frontend systems that make the product faster, clearer, and easier to iterate. The strongest example is the signup work, where iterative UI experiments improved conversion by 11 percent. For a recruiter, the evidence is not a list of tools, but shipped interface work tied to speed, consistency, and conversion. I would treat analytics instrumentation as a learning area rather than claim ownership of it, while bringing a grounded React, TypeScript, performance, and product-design collaboration base.

Sincerely,
Alex Martin

# 30 Universal Product Names

Names that read clearly across languages, feel premium, and avoid cultural friction.

---

**Names evoking craft and composition**

1. **Folio** — Latin root, understood globally. A curated collection of one's best work. Editorial, timeless.
2. **Draft** — The act of shaping something. Implies iteration and care. Every language has a creative equivalent.
3. **Nota** — Latin for "mark" or "note." Musical, short, universally legible. Works in Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, English.
4. **Opus** — A major creative work. Latin, used worldwide. Positions your career story as something composed, not just listed.
5. **Verso** — The reverse side of a page. Suggests there's more to you than what's on the surface. Romance-language friendly.
6. **Quill** — The original writing instrument. Immediately understood, evokes craftsmanship and authorship.

**Names evoking polish and presentation**

7. **Lustre** — Sheen, brilliance, the final polish. Spelled similarly across many European languages.
8. **Facet** — One polished surface of a gem. Suggests showing your best angle. Works in English, French, and beyond.
9. **Patina** — The beauty that comes from careful refinement. Universal word, warm and sophisticated.
10. **Gloss** — A clean, finished surface. Also means annotation or explanation. Short, punchy, premium.

**Names evoking structure and clarity**

11. **Contour** — A defining outline. Works across English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, German. Clean and visual.
12. **Profile** — Exactly what the product builds, elevated. Universal, clear, but needs strong branding to own it.
13. **Format** — Understood in virtually every language. Implies structure, presentation, and intentional design.
14. **Cadence** — Rhythm and flow. Suggests your career narrative has a natural momentum. Recognized globally.
15. **Schema** — A structured plan or framework. Greek root, used in dozens of languages. Intellectual and precise.

**Names evoking introductions and first impressions**

16. **Overture** — The opening piece. Musical, elegant, signals a confident beginning. Known in most Western languages.
17. **Prologue** — The text before the main story. Positions every application as the start of something bigger.
18. **Debut** — Your entrance. Used identically in English, French, and understood almost everywhere.
19. **Prima** — Latin for "first." Suggests first impression and first choice. Clean, strong, feminine energy without exclusion.

**Names evoking quiet authority**

20. **Gravitas** — Weight, seriousness, presence. Latin, universally understood among educated audiences worldwide.
21. **Calibre** — Quality and capability. Recognized across English, French, Spanish, Portuguese.
22. **Stature** — Professional standing and presence. Clear in English, close cognates everywhere.
23. **Merit** — Worth proven through quality. Simple, dignified, universal. Works in nearly every European language.
24. **Tenor** — The prevailing character or tone. Musical origin, globally familiar, suggests a consistent voice.

**Short, abstract, and globally brandable**

25. **Velum** — Fine parchment for important documents. Latin, rare in tech, tactile and distinctive.
26. **Lumen** — Latin for light. Suggests clarity, illumination, making your strengths visible. Universally recognized.
27. **Prism** — Light passes through and reveals its full spectrum. Your career, shown in all its dimensions.
28. **Signet** — A personal seal of authenticity. Implies something official, curated, and uniquely yours.
29. **Stem** — The core from which everything grows. Simple, organic, memorable. Four letters.
30. **Arc** — A career arc, a narrative arc. Three letters. Universally understood. Implies trajectory and story.

8. **Gravitas** — For a premium positioning. Promises the user they'll be taken seriously. Universally understood in professional contexts.
9. **Calibre** — Communicates high standards without arrogance. Natural in English, French, Spanish, Portuguese.

# 5 Naming Directions

**1. The Latin Universal** (Folio, Opus, Velum, Lumen, Nota)
Latin-root words function as a global lingua franca. They feel intellectual without being academic, premium without being exclusive. This direction gives you instant international legibility and a timeless quality that ages well.

**2. The Narrative Arc** (Arc, Overture, Prologue, Cadence, Debut)
Name the product after storytelling and momentum. This positions career-building as authorship — you're not filling forms, you're shaping a narrative. Appeals strongly to anyone who feels their CV doesn't capture who they really are.

**3. The Precision Object** (Prism, Facet, Signet, Contour, Schema)
Name something with geometry, clarity, and defined edges. These words suggest the product takes something blurry (your experience) and gives it sharp, visible structure. Works well for a design-forward brand.

**4. The Quiet Authority** (Gravitas, Calibre, Merit, Stature, Tenor)
Name the outcome the user wants: to be taken seriously. These words carry weight without shouting. Best for a premium tier targeting senior professionals, consultants, or executives.

**5. The Craftsperson's Tool** (Quill, Draft, Format, Gloss, Patina)
Name the act of making. This direction says the product is a workshop, not a template factory. It respects the user's intelligence and effort. Most approachable direction, works well for freelancers and creative professionals.
